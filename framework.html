The Framework

A commercial intelligence system for developing partnerships.

Most organisations approach partnerships from the outside.

They start with:

"Who can sponsor us?"

Framework starts earlier.

"What do we actually have?"

"Who values it?"

"Why?"

"What could be built around it?"

The Framework Method is our proprietary approach to answering those questions.

The Framework Method

Ten steps. One intelligence system.

We start with understanding. We end with a commercial blueprint.

01

Context

Understand the environment.

Before identifying an opportunity, we need to understand the environment in which it exists.

  • Market
  • Organisation
  • Objectives
  • Competition
  • Timing
  • Cultural and commercial context
02

Audience

Understand who actually matters.

The objective is to understand the commercial architecture of the audience.

  • Demographics
  • Psychographics
  • Behaviour
  • Needs
  • Attention
  • Influence
  • Purchasing behaviour
  • Identity
  • Access
03

Asset

Discover what actually exists.

Many organisations underestimate the number of commercially relevant assets they already possess.

  • Physical assets
  • Digital assets
  • Human assets
  • Content assets
  • Experiential assets
  • Intellectual assets
  • Access
  • Distribution
  • Timing
04

Value

Determine why the asset matters.

An asset only becomes valuable when we understand the value behind it.

  • Reach
  • Relevance
  • Attention
  • Trust
  • Access
  • Context
  • Exclusivity
  • Frequency
  • Cultural relevance
  • Commercial relevance
05

Fit

Find the organisations that have a reason to care.

The result is a Partner Fit Matrix, not a random list of brands.

  • Audience relevance
  • Brand alignment
  • Strategic objectives
  • Product relevance
  • Distribution
  • Activation potential
  • Timing
  • Geography
  • Exclusivity
  • Strategic value
06

Proposition

Build something worth buying.

We turn the opportunity into a commercial proposition.

  • What is being offered?
  • Why does it matter?
  • Who is it for?
  • What does participation look like?
  • What does the audience receive?
  • What does the organisation receive?
  • Why should the partner invest?
07

Partnership

Structure the relationship.

The objective is a relationship that creates value for the relevant parties.

  • Rights
  • Access
  • Exclusivity
  • Integration
  • Content
  • Experience
  • Distribution
  • Data considerations
  • Responsibilities
  • Governance
08

Activation

Make the partnership tangible.

We design the audience journey: discover → engage → experience → share → continue.

  • Discover
  • Engage
  • Experience
  • Share
  • Continue
09

Economics

Make the opportunity commercially viable.

The question is not simply "What can we charge?" It is "What is the economic architecture of this opportunity?"

  • Partner investment
  • Asset value
  • Activation cost
  • Delivery cost
  • Framework economics
  • Organisation value
  • Specialist costs
  • Long-term potential
10

Measurement

Define value before measuring it.

The metric follows the objective.

  • Reach
  • Attention
  • Engagement
  • Participation
  • Awareness
  • Consideration
  • Leads
  • Trial
  • Sales
  • Revenue
